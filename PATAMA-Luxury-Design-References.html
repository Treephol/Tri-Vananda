Ten ways to look
genuinely luxurious.

Ten distinct design languages from houses that define high-end taste — across fashion, hospitality, beauty and publishing. Each panel is an original specimen built to evoke the brand's design world (not its logo), so you can feel the difference side by side. Every direction would suit a wellbeing ecosystem differently.

Tell me the number (or two) that feels right for PATAMA — we can also blend, e.g. "Aman structure with Cucinelli warmth" — and I'll develop it into the teaser and the book.
01
Aman
Sanctuary Minimalism
Codes: near-black and stone, vast negative space, tiny wide-tracked sans, a single hairline, photography of light and emptiness. Luxury expressed by what is withheld.
For PATAMA — the calmest, most "retreat" option. Reads instantly as ultra-premium; risk is feeling cold for a multigenerational, joyful brand.

02
Loro Piana
Quiet Material Luxury
Codes: natural colourways drawn from the landscape, soft serif, almost no logo, the material itself as hero. The substance is the marketing.
For PATAMA — lets nature, forest and craft carry the brand. Understated wealth; pairs beautifully with the net-zero, farm-to-villa story.

03
The Row
Architectural Restraint
Codes: bone and charcoal, tone-on-tone, modern high-contrast serif used sparingly, garments and rooms like architecture. Silence as confidence.
For PATAMA — the most fashion-led, intellectual option. Elegant and contemporary; very urban, less "nature."
Tone on tone
THE ROW
precision · privacy
04
Brunello Cucinelli
Humanist Italian Warmth
Codes: warm stone and cream, classical serif, words about dignity and beauty, a sense of soul and craftsmanship. Luxury with a conscience.
For PATAMA — arguably the closest in spirit: warm, human, multigenerational, values-led. Wellbeing as a humane philosophy, not a clinic.
Humanistic luxury

05
Aesop
Intellectual Apothecary
Codes: dark olive and amber, dense typographic labels, literary copy, no faces or miracle claims. A skincare brand that reads like a library.
For PATAMA — perfect for the wellness products and the "at home" thread. Cerebral and precise; would need warmth added for the family story.

06
Hermès
Playful Craft Heritage
Codes: a confident signature colour, witty serif headlines, illustration and craft, a sense of joy and play atop deep savoir-faire.
For PATAMA — the most joyful option, and the best fit for "Family Joy." Heritage that smiles; needs discipline so it stays elevated.

07
Cartier × Assouline
Archival Grandeur
Codes: deep jewel tones, gold, high-contrast didone serif, framed museum plates, the icon paraded with reverence. The archive as the argument.
For PATAMA — the most overtly opulent; ideal for the coffee-table book and legacy storytelling. Risk: too "maison," not enough nature.
CARTIER
the impossible collection
08
Cereal / Kinfolk
Editorial Slow-Living
Codes: muted greys and greens, a strict grid, sans labels with serif headlines, enormous white space, page numbers. Travel-magazine calm.
For PATAMA — a natural home for the teaser and the book: editorial, modern, restful. Contemporary without shouting; very on-trend for wellness.

09
Bottega Veneta
Confident Modern
Codes: a single signature green, cream, bold clean sans, no visible logo — the technique is the signature. Quiet power, modern and sure.
For PATAMA — boldest and most contemporary. Strong, ownable colour; would feel fashion-forward rather than restful or natural.

10
Six Senses
Eco-Chic Nature
Codes: forest greens and sand, organic textures, light airy sans, photography where nature leads and architecture steps back. Mindful, of its place.
For PATAMA — the most directly "wellness + nature" fit, and closest to peers. Safe and credible; risk is looking like the category, not above it.
