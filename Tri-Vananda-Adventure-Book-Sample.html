Chapter One

A New Home Under the Mango Trees

Nara pressed her nose to the car window. The road had been winding for a long time, and she had almost fallen asleep.

Then her father said, "We're here."

She looked up — and there was a tree. The biggest tree she had ever seen. It was full of mangoes, each one glowing like a tiny sun. She opened the door, stepped out, and breathed in for the first time in what felt like a very long time.

The air smelled like green. It smelled, she thought, like the start of something.

CHAPTER ONE · CONT. PART I · BUILD
a very small visitor

Her little sister Ploy was already running.

"Nara! Nara! Come and see!"

Ploy was crouched in the garden, staring at a leaf as if it were the most important leaf in the world. Nara bent down. On the leaf was a frog. A very small frog. A perfectly green frog.

It looked at Ploy. Ploy looked at it.

"I think," said Ploy in her most serious voice, "that he is trying to tell me something."

CHAPTER TWO PART I · BUILD
Chapter Two

A Boy From the Village

The next morning, someone was at the gate.

A boy on a bicycle. His hair was messy, his feet were bare, and his basket was full of mangoes.

"I'm Arun," he said, holding one out.

Nara took it carefully, as if it might be a gift made of glass.

"You live here now?" asked Arun.

"I think so," said Nara.

Arun grinned. "Then I can show you the lakes."

CHAPTER TWO · CONT. PART II · BELONG
the secret of the nine lakes

They ran. Past the tamarind tree, past the mango grove, along a path lined with tall ferns that bent to touch their shoulders.

Then the trees opened — and there it was.

A lake. Then another. And another.

"There are nine of them," said Arun.

Ploy's mouth fell open. "Nine?"

"Nine," said Arun. And a hornbill, as though answering him, flew over their heads with a sound like wings clapping.

CHAPTER THREE PART I · BUILD
Chapter Three

The Morning Stretch Under the Tamarind

Under the oldest tamarind tree, an old man was stretching. His arms reached up slowly, as if he were a tree himself.

"Sawasdee," he said, smiling.

"Sawasdee," the children said back.

"I am Grandpa Tong. Would you like to try?"

He lifted his arms again. The three children stood in a line and lifted theirs.

"Roots down," said Grandpa Tong softly. "Branches up."

Somewhere, far away, a bird was singing a morning song.

Chapter Four

The Leaf That Whispered

It happened near the fifth lake.

A leaf came spinning down from the sky. Ploy caught it.

"Look," she whispered. "Look."

On the leaf, in tiny, careful handwriting, was a riddle. The children looked up. A hornbill — the same one? — was watching them from a branch.

"That's Kai," whispered Arun. "Kai leaves messages."

CHAPTER FOUR · CONT. PART II · BELONG
a plan, whispered

They decided: they would do it. They would come back tonight, when the moon met the water, and they would listen for the footsteps that were not their own.

"We have to be very quiet," said Nara.

"Quieter than a frog," said Ploy.

Tua-Tua, who had appeared on Nara's shoe without anyone noticing, said nothing at all — which is exactly what a frog is supposed to do.

"Then it's decided," said Arun. And somewhere in the forest, a branch moved — though no wind had asked it to.
