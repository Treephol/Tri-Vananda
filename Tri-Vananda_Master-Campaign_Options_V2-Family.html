IOption One · The legacy story

The Living Inheritance. What we pass on.

Wealthy families plan the transfer of everything — assets, houses, boards. Except the one inheritance that decides how all the rest is enjoyed.

Public campaign names

The Living Inheritance
Alternates: What We Pass On · Worth Passing On
Ad-system adaptation: Live What You Leave

The single insight

People believe legacy is what is written in the will. But health, habits and shared time are the inheritance that determines whether any of the rest matters — and it can only be transferred while you are alive.

The single feeling

Devotion. The weight and warmth of realising the most valuable thing you can give your children is the way you live.

The idea in practice

Wellbeing framed as the family's true estate. The campaign borrows the language of wealth transfer and turns it human: assets that appreciate (Sunday breakfasts, morning walks, a grandmother's recipe), a portfolio measured in shared years. The residence becomes the legacy home — the place where the inheritance is practised, not just stored.

Signature executions

Campaign film "The Will" — a patriarch reads his true bequest: not money, but mornings · print series in balance-sheet language ("Asset: dinner together, appreciating annually") · GWS panel on health as generational wealth · residence sales narrative rebuilt around legacy, not lifestyle.

IIOption Two · The togetherness argument

More Years, Together. Whom are your years for?

The longevity industry sells extra years to individuals. Nobody asks the better question: whom are those years with?

Public campaign names

More Years, Together
Alternates: Whom Are Your Years For? · The Years, Together
Ad-system adaptation: Live Connected, Together (existing line — the campaign gives it new depth)

The single insight

The tension is — every wellness destination separates the family to serve it: mother to the spa, children to the kids' club, grandfather to the clinic. The industry optimises individuals and abandons the unit that makes long life worth living.

The single feeling

Warmth. The exhale of everyone under one roof — three generations, one table, nobody checking the time.

The idea in practice

Position the estate as the answer the longevity movement forgot: the first wellness community designed for the whole family at once. Longevity is reframed as shared time — the point of more years is more years with each other. Every proof point already exists: multigenerational programming, the farm, the lakes, homes built for three generations.

Signature executions

Manifesto film shot around one long table, ages seven to seventy · "Whom are your years for?" — founder essay and GWS keynote provocation · social series: real three-generation days on the estate, documentary not staged · hosted GWS experiences run family-style, never conference-style.

IIIOption Three · The provenance story

Built for Our Own. From our family to yours.

Before this was a destination, it was one family's answer to illness — built for their own, then opened to those who live the same way.

Public campaign names

Built for Our Own
Alternates: From Our Family to Yours · A Family's Answer
Ad-system adaptation: Live Like Family

The single insight

People trust what a family builds for itself more than anything a company sells to strangers. In a category of invented wellness brands, a real origin — illness, recovery, three sons in the name — is the one asset that cannot be copied.

The single feeling

Trust. The difference between checking into a resort and being welcomed into a household.

The idea in practice

The founder family's journey told plainly and generously — the illness that started it, the healing that reshaped how they live, the estate built so their children and grandchildren could keep it. Every brand becomes a room in the household: the clinic they chose for their own parents, the restaurant that feeds them, the community they invited in. The anti-launch: discovery, never announcement.

Signature executions

Documentary film series — the family, the land, the practice · founder-told story placed with the incoming U.S. PR partner as their launch brief · guest communications and collateral signed "from our family to yours" · intimate family-hosted dinners as the campaign's true media.

A note on choosing

One family, three tenses.
Future, present, past.

The three options are the same family truth in three tenses. The Living Inheritance is the future — what will be passed on. More Years, Together is the present — the shared time happening now. Built for Our Own is the past — the origin that makes it all credible.

They nest naturally: lead the 2026 season with More Years, Together — it is the most newsworthy, it lands exactly on CLP + GWS, and the existing "Live Connected, Together" line already belongs to it. Run Built for Our Own beneath it as the proof layer for the U.S. PR launch, and hold The Living Inheritance as the residence-sales narrative it was born to be.
