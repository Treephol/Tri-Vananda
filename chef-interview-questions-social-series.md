# Chef Interview — Connected Storytelling Question Set
**Tri Vananda · Social content series · Documentary tone, no selling**

**How to use:** Each section flows into the next — the last question of each block opens the door to the following one. Ask the chef to include the question in their answer (for clean standalone clips). Aim for answers of 20–40 seconds. Let silence sit; the best lines come after the pause.

---

## 1. Introduction — *the person before the chef*

1. Tell us who you are — not your title. Who are you when you're not in the kitchen?
2. What's the first food memory you can still taste?
3. When did cooking stop being a job for you and become something else?
4. *Bridge:* What brought you here, to this land in Phuket?

> **Listen for:** a childhood detail, a family name, a specific dish. Specifics travel; titles don't.

---

## 2. Purpose — *why this kitchen exists*

1. What is this kitchen actually for? Beyond feeding people.
2. What do you want someone to feel after a meal here — not think, feel?
3. Is there something about how most people eat today that you're quietly pushing against?
4. *Bridge:* So if the purpose starts before the kitchen — where does a dish here actually begin?

> **Listen for:** conviction in plain words. If the answer sounds like a brochure, ask: "Say that again the way you'd tell your mother."

---

## 3. From Local Sources to Plate — *the journey of one ingredient*

1. Pick one ingredient in your kitchen right now. Walk us through its whole journey — soil to plate.
2. Who grows for you? Tell us about one farmer or fisherman by name.
3. What can this land give you that nothing imported ever could?
4. What's in season this exact week — and what does that decide for you?
5. *Bridge:* Every ingredient carries a story. Is there one dish here whose story means the most to you?

> **Listen for:** names, places, distances, seasons. "The morning glory came from Khun ___'s farm, twenty minutes away" is the clip.

---

## 4. Story Behind — *one dish, one memory*

1. Tell us the story behind that dish. Where does it come from — whose hands, whose memory?
2. Was there a moment a guest, a farmer, or a grandmother changed how you cook something?
3. What's a dish you make here that you couldn't make anywhere else in the world?
4. *Bridge:* These stories don't end at the plate. What happens after — to the scraps, the peels, the leftovers?

> **Listen for:** a single scene — a person, a place, a moment. Cut the answer to that scene.

---

## 5. Full Sustainable Ecosystem — *the circle, not the buzzword*

1. Follow the food past the plate for us. Where do the peels, bones, and trimmings go?
2. Show us the loop — what leaves the kitchen and comes back as soil, and what grows from it?
3. What does "nothing wasted" look like on an ordinary Tuesday, when no one is watching?
4. What's the hardest part of cooking this way? Be honest.
5. *Bridge:* It's easy to talk about philosophy. Where does it get tested in real life?

> **Listen for:** honesty about difficulty. The admission ("it's harder, it's slower") is what makes the rest believable.

---

## 6. From Philosophy to Real World — *where belief meets the daily grind*

1. When did this way of cooking cost you something — time, money, convenience — and you did it anyway?
2. How do you teach this to a young cook who just wants to move fast?
3. Have you ever compromised? What did you learn from it?
4. What's one small change from this kitchen that anyone watching could do at home tomorrow?

> **Listen for:** the trade-off and the practical takeaway. The home-kitchen tip is your most shareable clip.

---

## 7. Closing — *what stays with you*

1. When someone eats here and goes home — what do you hope stays with them?
2. In one sentence: what does food mean at Tri Vananda?
3. What are you still learning from this land?

> **Listen for:** the quiet line. This is your series opener or closer — often the best 10 seconds of the whole shoot.

---

## Filming notes for social

- **One section = one clip theme.** Seven sections → a 7-part series, or cut across sections by ingredient.
- **B-roll to capture while answers are fresh:** hands in soil, the walk from garden to kitchen, the named farmer, the compost loop, one dish plated in natural light.
- **Ask everything twice** if the first answer is polished. Take two is usually truer.
- **No calls to action on camera.** If it sounds like an ad, re-ask the question smaller.
